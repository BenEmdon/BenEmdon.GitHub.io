Agent approvals

October 2026

This series is about one thing: how we get to the next magnitude of agent scale.

Thesis

Approvals were designed for humans reading lines of code. When agents author most changes, approvals have to become semantic policy plus intent review. That shift is what gets us to the next magnitude of agent scale.

Where we are

Every change funnels through the same human review gate no matter what it is. A comment-only cleanup and a change to sensitive logic get the same treatment. Reviewers drown, agents wait, and the gate teaches us nothing about actual risk. We are spending our scarcest resource, human attention, completely flat.

The bet

The unit of approval should be what a change means, not who wrote it. Anthropic’s review tooling already expresses this:1 semantic constraints that separate comment-only changes from sensitive logical ones, so human reviewers focus on the consequential set. I want to take it further:

  • Approval policy as code. Rules over the change itself: what paths it touches, what behavior it alters, what risk class it falls in. Directory-level access control baked into source control, so sensitive code carries stronger requirements by default.
  • The AI reviewer as a first-class approver for low-risk classes. This has been the open question on my site for years: where does AI code review earn enough trust to approve a change. My answer now: in narrow, measured risk classes, with sampled human audit keeping it honest. Trust is earned per class and revoked on evidence.
  • Humans review intent, not lines. Does this change do what the author meant? Should it exist at all? What is the blast radius and the rollback story? That is the only review that still needs a person.

None of this lowers the bar. It moves the bar to where the risk actually is, and it stops pretending a tired human skimming a 400-line diff was ever a safety property.

What we build

  1. Approval policies expressed as semantic rules over the change, replacing blanket human gates.
  2. The AI reviewer approving low-risk classes outright, with continuous calibration against human judgment and a permanent audit sample.
  3. Human review reserved for intent and consequence, with the tooling shaped around that job.
  4. Rich access control in source control itself, so the most sensitive paths are protected without losing the monorepo.

Open questions

  • How trust is measured, grown, and revoked, and who owns that loop.
  • Accountability: who answers when an agent-approved change breaks something.
  • Keeping the audit loop cheap enough to run forever, because the day it stops, the trust decays.
