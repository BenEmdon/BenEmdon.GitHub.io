> This is a sample post on an experiment branch. It exists to show how a post
> looks and reads in this design, nothing more.

Every few years the web decides personal sites should be rebuilt on whatever
is new, and every few years the people who keep the same plain site quietly
outlast them. The boring site has no build to break, no framework to upgrade,
and no dependencies with opinions about your content. It is HTML that a
browser will still read in twenty years.

There is a cost, and it is honesty. A static page cannot pretend to be an app,
so every flourish has to earn its bytes. The boot screen on this site is a
good example: it is theatre, and it knows it is theatre. It holds the screen
for two seconds, states nothing the page does not already say, and gets out of
the way. That is the whole trick. Anything heavier would need a reason.

The boring stack also changes how you write. When publishing is a text file
and a commit, the gap between a thought and a post is small enough that the
thought survives. Fancy tooling adds steps, and each step is a chance to
decide the post was not worth it. Most unwritten posts die in a dashboard
somewhere.

None of this means the tools are bad. It means the site is not the project.
The writing is the project, and the site is the shelf it sits on. Pick the
shelf that needs the least dusting.
